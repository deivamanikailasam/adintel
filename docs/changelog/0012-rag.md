# 0012 — Retrieval (RAG) and numeric grounding

**In one sentence:** agents can now look up the agency's own playbooks and past incidents before
answering — never another client's — and every number they write is checked against the database.

## What changed

- `rag/chunking.py` — splits documents into meaningful pieces without ever cutting a paragraph, so an
  instruction stays attached to its condition.
- `rag/embeddings.py`, `rag/rerank.py` — local embedding and reranking models (BGE via fastembed) or
  deterministic offline stand-ins for tests.
- `rag/bm25.py` + `rag/retriever.py` — **hybrid search**: meaning-based (vectors) plus exact-word
  (BM25), merged 65/35, top 25 re-ranked down to 6. The client filter is applied *inside* the search.
  Results are deterministic (ties broken by ID) and cached per client and brand.
- `rag/indexer.py` — indexing with upload sanitising, injection flagging, soft-delete on re-index and
  anonymisation of other brands' incidents ("a client in this vertical").
- `rag/narratives.py` — turns number tables into sentences before indexing (numbers are never embedded).
- `rag/grounding.py` — **numeric grounding**: every figure in generated text must match a figure from
  the database, or the text is blocked.

## Why it matters (layman's terms)

Recommendations have to come from *this* agency's experience, not generic marketing advice. Searching
by meaning alone misses exact ad-set names; searching by words alone misses questions like "why did
our costs go up". Using both, then re-reading the best candidates carefully, gets the right evidence.

And because language models can invent plausible numbers, a separate piece of plain code checks every
number before anything reaches a client.
